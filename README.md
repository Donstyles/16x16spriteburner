# Cellar of Ruin

One hero fights down an endless cellar, floor by floor. Steer it yourself, hand parts of the job to the hero, or bring up to three friends.

The main menu offers **PLAY SOLO**, **HOST CO-OP** and **JOIN CO-OP**, plus **HOW TO PLAY** and **TRAINING GROUND**. Your first solo game starts with a short hands-on tutorial (move, automatic attacks, casting, dodging, growing stronger); each step ends when you do it, SKIP ends it at any time, and HOW TO PLAY replays it. Enemies take it easy on you while the tutorial runs.

**TRAINING GROUND** is a solo sandbox for trying spells. Pick any hero; it arrives at level 12 with the first spell of every tier and its ultimate, facing a training dummy that never falls and never strikes back (it springs back to its post after knockbacks, tornadoes and freezes). **LOADOUT** (top right, or L) equips any basic attack, Low, Medium and High spell and switches each spell's three upgrades on and off. It also sets the level (1–20), switches between 1, 6 and 12 dummies, and turns the automatic basic attack off so spells can be measured alone. Cooldowns are real so the damage numbers mean something, but closing the loadout makes every spell ready again, the ultimate included; COOLDOWNS: OFF lets every spell fire nonstop. The bar at the top shows damage per second over the last five seconds, total damage and the biggest hit; tap it to reset. Nothing in the training ground counts toward a run.

## Spells and progression
Every hero holds **one basic attack, one Low, one Medium and one High spell, and one Ultimate**. Each is chosen once, from every spell of that tier, and kept for the whole run; nothing is ever replaced.

- **Before the first wave** you pick a hero, its basic attack (it fires by itself at the nearest foe) and its Low spell (button 1).
- **Levels 5, 9 and 12** open the Medium, High and Ultimate tiers. When that floor is cleared, the cards show every spell of the new tier: Medium goes on button 2, High on button 3, the Ultimate on its own button.
- **Every other cleared floor** (on boss floors: after the boss) offers three upgrades for spells you hold, basic attack included. Each spell has three upgrades; damage, size and duration upgrades multiply, extra projectiles or targets add up. Unpicked cards pick themselves after 18 seconds.
- **Levels** heal 35% and raise health and damage. Ignis, Kazimir, Seraphine, Nerida and Aldric take a new form at level 10, Voltaire at 12; Brandt and Mossa keep theirs.
- **Ultimates** have a 60-second cooldown and nothing else brings them back; a newly learned one is ready at once. Only one ultimate can run at a time: while a friend's is going, yours shows WAIT, so everyone gets to see each one.
- The SPELLBOOK (button, the hero frame, or B) shows all five tiers with every spell of the hero: yours lit with its upgrades, the rest dimmed, locked tiers with the level they open at. Tap a spell for details. The battle pauses while it is open.

## Controls
The game is always landscape. On a phone held upright it is drawn sideways, so just turn the phone (this works with rotation lock on).

**AUTO MOVE** walks the hero for you; you still dodge, and dragging takes over for a moment. **AUTOPILOT** walks and dodges by itself: it steps out of telegraphed slams, sidesteps shots about to land and breaks out when surrounded and badly hurt. **AUTO SPELLS** casts your spells for you. The spell buttons always work: tapping one fires it at once, whatever MOVEMENT and SPELLS are set to. If you don't touch anything for 10 seconds during a fight, autopilot with auto spells takes over until you do. MENU pauses a solo game; in co-op the fight goes on.

Tap once for sound. On Android the game also goes fullscreen and big hits vibrate.

## Co-op (up to 4 heroes)
Choose **HOST CO-OP** on the menu to open **The Cellar Door**, the lobby: a 4-letter room code, **SEND INVITE LINK**, and four seats that fill as friends arrive, each showing the hero that player picked. Friends tap the invite link, or **JOIN CO-OP** and type the code on the letter pad. When everyone is ready the host taps **DESCEND**; each player then picks a basic attack and a first spell. Friends who arrive later drop straight into the fight. A room can also be opened mid-run from **MENU → PLAY TOGETHER**.

- XP and levels are shared; each player makes their own picks after every floor and boss, and picking never pauses the fight.
- A fallen hero comes back after 9 seconds while anyone is still standing.
- If a guest's connection drops, their hero fights on by itself and they get it back when the page reconnects. The host's phone runs the game, so it must stay open in the foreground.
- The connection indicator under the top bar shows round trip, packet loss and sync state; tap it for details.
- Matchmaking uses the free public PeerJS server; phones then talk directly, or through a relay when a network blocks direct connections. Co-op runs on the GitHub Pages / Home Screen version, not inside claude.ai.

## Cellar of Ruin II (the successor, in progress)
A dungeon crawl in `crawler/` (on GitHub Pages at `/crawler/`), its own game: deliberate real-time combat, a dark dungeon you light yourself, and roguelite runs. It shares only the engine with the first game (pixel renderer and lighting, the dungeon builder and its wall logic, sprites, effects, sound).

- **The heroes' weapons, cut by hand.** Each hero's weapon is cut out of his sprite and swung on its own. Where a frame has been cut by hand in the Hero Cut Painter (an artifact: the weapon's pixels marked, the hero redrawn where it was, its grip set), the game uses that (`node tools/herocuts.js <dir>` turns the painter's saved frames into `11_herocuts.js`); any other frame is cut by the weapon's colours as before. The Lancer and the Gladiator are cut by hand.
- **The walls as painted** (`31_walls`). Every wall piece of the cellar sheet is painted pixel by pixel in the Wall & Trim Painter (an artifact; its text goes into the game with `node tools/paintwalls.js painted.json`). Each pixel is **wall**, where no foot ever stands, or else **floor**, and it may also be **trim**: the ceiling, drawn over everything (units, blood, sparks, glows). The camera looks north and down, so the top of a wall is drawn north of where the wall stands. A south wall's whole row is floor behind it, under its trim: you walk in behind the wall and it hides you, all but your head. An east or west wall is wall under its trim, with a strip of floor four pixels wide beside the room: you walk right up to its top, which covers whatever of you reaches past it. A north wall's bricks are wall without trim: you stand in front of them, drawn over them, but your shadow, blood and anything else lying flat never are (its top trim only keeps the glows off it). A piece's floor is floor only where the floor reaches it, pixel by pixel from the floor tiles beside it; a foot there counts as standing on that floor tile, sight and shots pass over it, and coins never slide in under the ceiling. The floor behind a wall is whatever lies north of it: behind a pit or a pool the row is pit or water too, so nobody walks round a chasm or a flooded way behind the wall, and anything that blocks a corridor running east and west blocks that row as well. A pillar stands on a plinth: feet coming at it from the north stop where the plinth's top begins, never sinking in behind it, and the plinth is low, so whoever stands beside it, and his weapon, is drawn over it; the column above it is in front of or behind him by its middle.
- **The dungeon is dark.** Your lamp and the wall torches light it; what you have seen stays faintly visible, what you have not stays black, and foes only show where you can see them. A map in the corner fills in as you go. The way down is in the farthest room.
- **Combat is by hand.** Tap ATTACK to strike and keep tapping to chain the weapon's combo; hold it to charge a heavy blow. ROLL tumbles you out of the way, untouchable for most of it, and held on with a direction it becomes a sprint (half as fast again, costing 33 stamina a second, your footfalls pounding seven a second); BLOCK soaks blows for stamina, and raised just as a blow lands it parries, leaving the attacker staggered and open to a riposte. Every move costs stamina; spend the very last of it and you are winded: nothing comes back for 1.6 s, you can only trudge (40% speed, picking up as the stamina returns), you breathe hard through the nose, and sweat runs down your head and drips onto the stone. Holding block, the hero holds his weapon across his front just inside the guard's arc, between himself and it. Hitting a sleeping or unaware foe is a sneak attack; hitting one from behind is a backstab. Three flasks heal.
- **Kits:** every hero's weapon is cut out of his own sprite (found by its colours) and really swung: drawn back, swept through the arc, thrust, spun, slammed, raised back to charge, turned about the hand with RotSprite so the blade keeps clean pixels. Sellsword (sword and shield), Lancer (halberd and shield), Gladiator (two-handed club), Cutthroat (twin daggers, rolls far, deadly from behind). Each weapon has its own combo and heavy blow.
- **Foes** fight in four ways and together. Rushers (grunts, skitterers) run straight in and swarm. Tanks (brutes) throw you back with a wide shove, every third blow a slam. Squads: each tank shields up to three archers (shared out evenly); the tank stands between you and its archers and holds its ground (it steps across to stay in your way, never back, and shoves you off when you try to get past to them), the archers keep their distance in a loose line behind it and take turns to shoot (a steady stream, aimed a little ahead of you), and a second squad takes up a bearing a quarter turn round from the first, so the arrows come from two sides. Archers and casters with no tank work as a pair: they hold bearings a quarter turn apart round you (going round you to get there), so you cannot face both at once; a third or fourth takes the next quarter. Their arrowheads glint as they fly, a bright streak that twinkles a few times a second, even beyond the lamp's reach. Archers loose only along a clear line (pillars stop arrows exactly where their stone is drawn). Flankers circle wide to your back while you are busy with the others, stab and get out; turn to face them and they peel off. Bats fly: they hover over their shadows, rising and sinking gently and drifting in slow loops, turn in wide arcs, climb as they wind up and dive at you to strike; asleep they rest on the floor and take off when woken, and killed they fall out of the air. Every attack is marked on the floor in red before it lands. Awake foes see ahead of them and hear all round; sleepers lie on their backs and only hear, and spring up when startled; a foe that loses you searches where it last saw you and in the end goes home. Each floor is held by one army, named on the floor's card, and every foe on it comes from that army; an army that lacks a role fields more of its others. The cellars (floors 1–3): the goblin warband, the cellar vermin (no archers) or the ant colony (no flankers). The crypt (4–6): the bone legion (its brute a grappler mummy) or the rot, which often sends its mutilated stumblers as a horde of four to six. The undead (bones, revenants, mummies, the skeletal archers and crawlers, stumblers, ghouls, cadavers, the flesh golem, rotting archers, dismembered crawlers) never sleep: found idle they stand about or wander their room. The necromancers, witches, bats and maggots are living and may sleep. The deep (7 on): the lizardfolk tribe, the merfolk raiders, the elven host or the demon horde (its brute the blood drinker). The armies are laid out on an army board (a separate page with every creature of the Creature Compendium on it) and copied into the game from there. A floor is never held by the same army as the one above, where the depth has another. Every treasure room has a guardian standing by its chest: the scarecrow in the cellars, a stone or clay golem deeper down. Kept in reserve, on no floor yet: the king's soldiers, the wanderers (the minotaur and the toxic hound), the cackling pack (gnolls), the tinkers' guild (gnomes and a mithril construct), the plague nest (ratkin and their rats), the kobold warren and the skullsplitter clan (orcs).
- **The hero's panel** (top left): his portrait in a little lamp-lit frame (a close-up of his face, four times over, found from his sprite and breathing in its idle frames), his name in the display face, his health and stamina as bevelled bars with a notch every 25 health and the part just lost lingering pale before it drains, and his flasks as the flask from the item sheet, full or empty. Low on health the frame and the bar beat red with his heart; cursed, the stamina bar turns violet; out of breath, it flashes.
- **A floor** is nine rooms on the first, one more every second floor down, up to fourteen, on a grid five rooms wide and four deep: the way down winds through about two thirds of them (five rooms from the start to the stairs on the first, seven or eight deep down), the rest hang off it as side rooms, and a loop or two joins rooms up but never cuts the way short. The minimap stays its size in the corner.
- **Room shapes** (`33_shapes`). A third of the rooms (more the deeper you go, up to six in ten) are given a shape of their own before the walls are painted, so their walls are drawn from the sheet's pieces like any other: **the pillared hall**, blocks of wall standing free in the room, two tiles wide and three tall (two tall, the cap sits straight on the bricks and the sheet's cap piece shows floor through it), in a pair, a four or two rows of three, mirrored about its middle; **the ring**, one great block of wall in the middle, square: three or five wide (odd, like the corridors) and a tile taller, centred on the line of a north or south doorway so it lines up with the corridors, so its top (drawn north of where it stands) is as deep as it is wide and its bricks are the extra row, the room a walk round it; **cut corners**, one, two or all four corners of the rock left standing (an L, a Z, a cross); **alcoves**, niches let into the north wall (or a side wall without a door) holding a round column on its plinth, a burning brazier, a small chest or a column, a torch on the niche's back wall (either side of a column); **spurs**, walls run out into the room from a side, making bays (from the north or south three tiles thick; from the east or west four, so their tops read two tiles wide, a short stub of two tiles staying three); and **the serpentine**, spurs from the north and the south in turn, so the way through winds. The blocks a shape leaves standing are decorated like walls where they have plain brick (torches, banners, barred windows): a 2-wide block has none, a 3-wide ring's middle takes a torch, a banner or a window, a 5-wide ring's face a torch, a banner or a window and a torch (or a banner alone, two windows or two torches), a spur's or a cut corner's long side torches four tiles apart about its middle with now and then a banner or a window between; in a dark room they are out like its others. The top of a block, like a south wall's, is drawn north of where it stands: you can walk in behind it, all but your head hidden. Every door still reaches every other, no lane is a single tile wide and the doors' landings stay clear; a shaped room takes no set piece, pit, cistern, shrine or pond (its walls are its feature), and the end room may have a pillared hall or a ring, kept clear of its stairs. Torches, banners and windows hang only on plain north wall: never on a face's end or the corner beside an alcove, a cut corner or a doorway (a tall window or banner reaches up into the wall's top, and would run over the corner).
- **Great halls and corridors** (`30_arena`, `33_shapes`). From the second floor, about every other floor (two now and then from the sixth) **a great hall** is made of two neighbouring cells of the grid: a long hall thirty to thirty-six tiles across, or a nave twenty-four to twenty-eight tall, covering both cells' middles so the corridors still meet it square; the end room may be one. Its colonnade: blocks of wall two tiles wide, every five or six tiles, in two rows with a nave between them (or one row down the middle where two do not fit), or two columns down a nave; any that would stand in a doorway's way is left out (in the crypt the colonnade is laid out from the hall's middle in mirrored pairs, and a block's mirror is left out with it, so the hall stays symmetric). It draws more foes than a room (up to two and a half times). Corridors still run along the cells' middle lines, but about one in five **bends**: it jogs aside halfway and comes into the far room three or four tiles off its middle (never into the shop). About one in five is **a gallery**: twice as wide between its doorways, its doorways as wide as ever, with columns down its middle every three tiles; running north and south it widens only three tiles clear of the rooms at each end, so there are three rows of wall between it and either room (a wall one tile thick has no piece in the sheet). Neither takes spikes, barricades or clutter.
- **What a floor is like** (`35_themes`). Each floor is given a character, named on its card: plain (most often), **the storehouses** (warehouses, potteries and cart sheds wherever a room is free), **the halls of stone** (shaped rooms and great halls far more often, colonnades), **the lightless** (half its rooms with their torches and braziers dead), **the waterworks** (a cistern wherever one can go, more ponds, the flooded way more often), **the broken floors** (two more kinds of pit) or **the locked ways** (one more puzzle, more cracked walls); the first floor is plain or one of the first three. Deeper down, now and then a room is dark anyway. **Room themes**, for a room with no set piece, water, shrine, sluice or puzzle (some shapes take some of them, a great hall three times as often): **the warehouse**, rows of crate stacks and crates the way the doors leave most room for, a narrow aisle between each two, a gap or two in every row and the doors' ways clear (it all breaks); **the pottery**, islands of pots; **the cart shed**, mine carts nose to tail in rows, junk about them; **the colonnade**, round columns on their plinths in a row along the north and the south facing an aisle, a burning brazier at each end. **The cold braziers** (a puzzle, `41_puzzles`): the room before a gate is dark and three or four braziers stand cold about it (in the crypt in mirrored pairs across the room, the pair farthest from those already standing first), dead coals in each (every brazier with coals in it can be lit, anywhere, the same way, a dark rune room's or a drained cistern's too, and burns on; the dark rooms of the lightless floors, which stay dark, have empty ones); stand by one a while (after a second it starts to smoke, after two and a half it catches: nobody lights one by brushing past) and it lights, burns thirty seconds, sputters, and goes out; all of them burning at once and beams of fire run to the gate and it opens.
- **The night gallery** (`65_gallery`; TEST ROOMS, then NIGHT GALLERY). A grid of every room shape, corridor, theme, puzzle and kind of floor made in the night of the long build; picking one builds a floor with it on, takes the foes away and puts the hero right by it, with a card naming it and saying how it works; its last column (in blood red) is the catacombs: TOMB HALL, BURIAL CHAMBER, CHARNEL PIT, SKULL FONT, THRONE CRYPT, THE SHADE, NICHE AMBUSH, THE BURIED, BLOOD SEAL, THE VIGIL, BLOOD CISTERN, BURIAL GALLERY, MOSSY DEEP, each built as a crypt floor (the sixth for the mossy deep), where the dead that are the thing to see stay (the Shade, the lord on his throne, the buried); PREV and NEXT at the top of the screen step through them all in order (wrapping round), its number and name under them. **Every floor can be solved**: a set piece's sluice or button is never put behind a gate, gate keys are always before their gate, and a room with a door off its middle line (a bent corridor's) takes no gauntlet and no ponds. Checked by solving five hundred floors on paper (from the spawn, open whatever can be reached, again and again, until the stairs): none was stuck, and nearly all took two or more steps.
- **Seeds and the report.** Every run has a seed (shown tiny in the bottom right corner with the version, always, so any screenshot says which floor it is; and in the pause menu under the buttons, with the floor), and each floor is made from a stream of random numbers of its own, from that seed and the floor's number, so the same seed and floor give the same floor again in the same version; play itself (blows, sparks, what creatures decide) stays random. LOG in the pause menu (the menu button, top right) lays out, as text to copy, the version, the seed, the floor, the army before it, where the hero is, how the way down, the gates, plates, water and cracked walls stand, which creatures are still on their feet and where, and the last forty things that happened (kills, plates, gates, walls broken, water drained, the way down unsealed). `CR.replayFloor({ seed, floor, prevArmy })` makes a reported floor again; a test (`determinism.js`) builds the same seeds in two page loads and compares the floors tile for tile and the painted ground and ceiling pixel for pixel. Nothing in the making of a floor shuffles by a random comparator (`sort(() => Math.random() - 0.5)`): how often a sort calls its comparator differs from engine to engine and could make the same seed give another floor after other floors had been played in the page (or on another browser); a shuffle (`pitShuffle`), then a stable sort, gives the same floor every time.
- **The catacombs** (floors 4 to 6; also from the test rooms screen: CATACOMBS, from the first floor): floors drawn in the crypt sheet (ElvGames' RA_Crypt, `crawler/assets/crypt*.png`), its walls, trim, brick faces and slab floors laid over the grey cellar, whose doors, water, props and every system keep working underneath: the crypt's pieces stand in for the cellar's by name (`THEMES.crypt.map` in `30_arena`, drawn through `srcOf`), so nothing that reads the walls changes. The crypt's walls are drawn the other way about from the cellar's (the dark top of the wall fills the tile, the trim runs along its room side, no strip of floor beside it), so until they are painted in the painter a crypt wall piece is ceiling wherever it is drawn and its side walls are wall all over (`31_walls`). With no ceiling trim to hide feet under, nobody steps into a crypt side wall, and the south wall keeps them off its last four rows. The crypt floor is laid by rule, nothing in it left to chance. Two stones: small bricks (four to a tile) and big slabs (plain, or quartered by grout lines). How a room is paved follows what it is: the way out and the great rooms a nave of quartered slabs framed by a row of bricks along the walls, the treasure rooms and middling rooms plain slabs wall to wall, small rooms and the corridors brick. The wear follows the feet: from every doorway (and the stairways, whose places are settled before the floor is laid) straight into its room and along to the middle (wider where paths meet), and down the middle of every corridor, the stone is worn away in a band that swells and narrows slowly along the way, a smooth function of the place so crossing paths agree; bricks go where the band reaches, a slab only where it runs deep (it outlasts a brick); rooms nobody passes through much (treasure, shop, secret rooms) are worn less. A slab corner is broken off where it points out into bare earth, and along a band's edge only where the wear has all but reached that corner itself (at most one corner of a slab along any one edge, the more worn), and never elsewhere (each chipped corner is that quarter of the sheet's chipped slabs, mirrored for the corners it lacks; its own grout opens into the dirt where earth lies beyond). Bare earth is plain but for its grit (hashed by place); the grout's print runs only along stone still down and opens into the dirt everywhere else. The cellar's own pieces that the crypt keeps (crates, pots, chests, braziers, pillars, the blast doors, plates, bridges, the water's rims) are drawn from recoloured sheets in the crypt's stone (`crawler/assets/*_crypt.png`, made by `tools/crypt_recolor.py`: every colour to the crypt stone nearest it in lightness, only water, fire, gold and blood kept), swapped in by the floor's theme (`themeSheets` in `30_arena`). The crypt has no decorative beams over its doorways (only a blast door keeps its frame). The crypt's pools hold no water (and its bridges are laid with the darker sandstone slats): each crypt floor's pools are blood (deep, old blood), the black blood of its dead (a red in its black, old blood) or the green rot of the undead. A pool is still read off the blue art (IMG.pool), then laid over in the liquid's eight shades by lightness; its glints, the whirl over the drain, the drops thrown up, the rings and the wet it leaves take the liquid's colours, and the floor's sheet has its water pieces (sunken pillars and the like) in the same liquid (`LIQUIDS` in `36_works`). The bare earth carries a few more specks of grit. Nothing in a crypt room is placed by chance (`43_crypt`). Its north wall, from the middle out every second tile and mirrored, alternates candle niches (the room's light) with niches of the dead (skulls, urns, chains in turn), a place used only when its mirror is free too; a run of plain wall that pass had to leave bare (a doorway or an alcove off the room's middle has no mirror) is dressed the same way from its own middle out. Braziers stand in a mirrored pair in a big room's north corners (both or neither: none where a doorway takes one corner). What stands on the floor goes in last of all, after the set pieces, pools and puzzles (`cryptDress`), only on plain floor off the worn paths, a tile or more from anything the floor already holds and two from a doorway; a room with blocks to push, a set piece, a pool or a shrine gets none, and each pair goes down only if every tile of the room reachable before is reachable after. A big room becomes a tomb hall: rows of tombs either side of its paths, one kind of tomb to a row (the back row the tall ones with headstones), the tombs nearest the aisle lit with candles; a row of fewer than two tombs is left out, and a hall that would hold fewer than four is dressed as a smaller room instead. - **Sound** wakes on any tap and whenever you come back to the game (an iPhone stops a page's sound when you switch apps and lets it start again only on a tap). If it stays silent, check the ring/silent switch: on silent an iPhone mutes web pages.
- **Sneaking.** Sleepers hear rather than see. Creep (push the invisible stick only a little, or hold C or Ctrl) and you can slip right past one: it wakes only if you bump into it. Walking wakes it from 34 px, rolling or fighting from 56. Creeping, the hero crouches (the upper body a pixel lower) and shades the lamp.
- **Stagger is not a chance.** Every foe has a hidden poise bar (grunt 55, archer 30, skitterer 12, brute 200) that each blow wears down (sword 10/10/24, heavy 60; halberd 9/9/22, heavy 55; club 28/36, heavy 95; daggers 4/4/4/10, heavy 24; sneak attacks, backstabs and ripostes ×1.5). It refills after 2 s without a hit; at zero the foe reels for 1.1 s and takes 30% more damage, and once back on its feet it cannot be worn down again for 2.5 s. Light blows rarely stagger on their own: finishers and heavies do. A parry always staggers, for 1.6 s. A brute cannot be worn down mid-slam. The hero has 30 poise, reels 0.45 s, then has 1.5 s of grace.
- **Blood.** It flies the way the blow went: a sword's sweep flings an arc of drops along the swing, a thrust (spear, a finisher, a dagger's stab) shoots a jet out of the far side, the axe throws heavy clots, daggers quick spurts, the overhead slam splashes all round; a foe's blow or an arrow bleeds the hero the same way. Half the drops fly as 2x2 clots (the axe throws more). Every drop lands as a soft blotch on the floor and stays. A drop that reaches a wall leaves a soft splash there (a north wall on its face, a side wall on its rim, a south wall against its edge); about half of them (nearly every clot) start a run that creeps down over 5 to 7 seconds, thinning, with a bead of blood at its front, and collects in a small blotch where it reaches the floor; a drop landing beside a run feeds it instead of starting another. Kills leave a pool. Each creature bleeds its own: red, green-yellow ichor (bugs), green slime (the maggot), black sludge (the crypt's dead flesh, the sand ghoul and the necromancer); skeletons, the scarecrow and the golems do not bleed (see below). Blood gathers on the hero's blade (patches from the tip down, in the victim's colour), drips from its tip and wears off over a minute; a roll wipes some away. Walk through a pool and you leave fading footprints. The badly wounded, hero or foe, leave a trail of drips. A wet squelch sits under every bleeding hit.
- **Overkill.** A killing blow that alone does more than half the foe's full health (whatever it had left) blows one of the bigger foes (archers, flankers, brutes; never the trash) apart: it bursts into chunks of its own sprite with raw red edges, which bleed as they fly and where they land, lie a few seconds and fade (no bodies stay). The moment is held: the hero freezes a little longer and the burst starts in slow motion before it flies.

- **Bottomless pits** (`38_pits`). Holes in the floor going down into the dark, from the sheet's own pit pieces: the earth under a floor's edge with roots hanging from it, fading to black, lies in the pit right under a floor (we look down past the floor's edge at its thickness), or under the foot of a north wall the pit runs up to; the rest of a pit is black, the floor beside it is seen edge on and the floor in front of it hides its far side (a side wall's strip of floor beside a pit, and the top of a south wall in front of one, go down into it too). Feet stop at the edge (it blocks like water, and sight, arrows and spells pass over it), and so do rolls and lunges; but a hard enough blow carries whoever takes it over the edge: a brute's shove or slam (straight away from the brute, so you go over only with the pit behind you), a heavy swing, a wave, a spike trap's throw. Who goes over falls: smaller and darker into the black, turning a little, sinking behind the floor in front of the hole, with a rush of air and, far below, a few stones still falling. A hero climbs back out where he last stood clear of every pit, a fifth of his health the poorer and blinking a moment; a fall he cannot pay for is his last (he is simply gone). No floor is ever left cut off from its room's doors, so no one is marooned: no layout leaves any (holes and the loose slabs beside them included); when a slab drops, any floor it leaves held by nothing (a slab with pit north, south, east and west, held only at its corners, or a patch of a few cut off together) cracks and falls a moment later, whoever stands on it going down with it; and should a hero still find himself penned in by pits (NO WAY OUT · STEP OFF), he may step off the edge, and climbs out inside the nearest door if where he last stood clear is itself cut off. The spot he climbs out at is kept on every floor (one whose only pits lie hidden under loose slabs too). A foe that falls is gone, its loot with it (it counts as a kill). Blood, gibs, sparks and stones that come down over a pit drop away into it and leave no mark; nothing painted on the floor ever shows on the void. A shadow lies only on the floor: it stops at a pit's edge, never on the earth face or the black, and never on a wall either (a north wall's bricks stand up from the floor's edge, so whoever stands at their foot is drawn over them but his shadow is not). Where a wall beside a pit leaves pixels open (a side wall's strip, the open corner under a cap), they are pit too, the earth face carried on into them. Pressing at an edge knocks crumbs off it. A room can have them seven ways (never the first room, the last, the treasure room or a set piece's, and every door stays joined to every other): **the chasm**, a band of pit two or three tiles wide from wall to wall, crossed by a bridge two tiles wide (the floor left standing, or half the time the sheet's wooden bridge, its planks fresh or green with age) with a brute standing guard on it; **broken floor**, holes here and there with cracked slabs round some of them (a crumb drops off one now and then: the tell) that shake and grind under a foot and drop away a moment later, taking whoever stands on them; **the ledges**, a room all pit but a platform in its middle with a brute on it and a way two tiles wide out to it from every door; **the crumbling floor**, a room with no hole in it as you come in, nearly every slab of it cracked: each gives way a moment after a foot comes down on it, so whoever keeps moving gets across with the floor falling in behind him, and whoever stops goes down; a winding way of sound slabs joins every door, looking like the rest (only the crumbs that drop now and then off the cracked ones tell them apart). A treasure room may be laid the same way, looking like any other, its sound ways leading to the chest and a little sound floor in front of it for the guardian (until it comes after you). Two more only give a fight an edge: **pit corners**, a hole two or three tiles square in two to four of a room's corners, and **the pit rim**, a pit a tile wide along every wall but where the doors come in; a shove, a slam or a heavy swing near the walls is a fall. A treasure room may have either (its chest and its guardian's floor stay). **The spiked walkway** (from the second floor): all pit but a walkway two tiles wide from every door to a junction in the middle (an L where a door does not line up with it), with a field of spike traps two or three deep across one of the ways; a spike throws whoever it stabs hard out of the nearer end of its field, along the way, and from the third floor down the field lies right before a turn, so the throw out of that end carries on over the corner into the pit, and a brute holds the junction. Cracked slabs lie still until a hero first comes into their room, and a heavy blow's cracks (a brute's slam's too) running over one set it crumbling. The first floor may have broken floor, a crumbling floor or pit corners; deeper floors a chasm or the ledges, and broken or crumbling floor, pit corners or the rim besides. ## Art rules (Cellar of Ruin II)

- **One edge for every item.** Every pickup, ware and item (the fish and flask, the chests, the gambeson, the parchments, scrolls and maps, anything new) is outlined in the same dark, faintly warm `#2b2522`: never stark black, never a tint of the item's own colour; the gambeson's quilting on the HUD is outlined in it too. An item or icon brought in from another pack has its outline (any dark pixel on its edge) recoloured to it before it goes on a sheet. Coins are exempt (drawn in code, a few gold pixels).
- **Pixel art at whole pixels.** Anything drawn for the game, HUD included, is drawn in art pixels on a grid and scaled up without smoothing, in colours taken from the sprites it belongs with, never as smooth gradients or hairlines.

## Run it
Open `index.html` (it is self-contained), or add the GitHub Pages site to your Home Screen. The sequel is `crawler/index.html`, with a web app manifest of its own (`crawler/manifest.webmanifest`: full screen, landscape), so on Android it installs from the browser's menu (Add to Home screen / Install app) and opens full screen; in a browser tab on Android it goes full screen on the first touch (the Fullscreen API; an iPhone has none for a page, there the Home Screen icon does it).

## Build
```
python3 -I tools/pack.py <extracted cellar tileset zip> <folder with every extracted creature pack>.
python3 -I tools/add_mobs.py <extracted Compendium/Animations folder>. Name [Name...]   # add Compendium creatures to the sheet
python3 -I tools/recolor.py   # vampire, priest, tidecaller, stormcaller, general and their evolved forms, the general's archers and riders, angels (palette swaps)
node tools/build.js
node tools/build.js crawler   # the sequel
```
`src/*.js` is concatenated into `index.html` (Pages / Home Screen) and `dist/artifact.html`; `crawler/src/*.js` into `crawler/index.html` and `crawler/dist/artifact.html` (it also inlines `crawler/assets/cellar_doors.png`).
Checks: `node tools/shots.js`, `node tools/scenes.js`, `node tools/soak.js` against a local server on port 8787; `node tools/soak2.js <seconds> <turbo> <kit>` plays the successor with its test pilot.

Art: the Dungeon Cellar tileset and its doors by ELV Games (rooms and props), and the 16x16 creature packs by Deep Dive Game Studio: Magical, Humanoid, Humanoid II, Vermin, Undead, Demon, Dragon and Holy, and more creatures from their Creature Compendium (heroes, monsters and angels; some heroes are palette swaps of their sprites).
