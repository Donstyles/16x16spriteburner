# Cellar of Ruin

One hero fights down an endless cellar, floor by floor. Steer it yourself, hand parts of the job to the hero, or bring up to three friends.

The main menu offers **PLAY SOLO**, **HOST CO-OP** and **JOIN CO-OP**, plus **HOW TO PLAY** and **TRAINING GROUND**. Your first solo game starts with a short hands-on tutorial (move, automatic attacks, casting, dodging, growing stronger); each step ends when you do it, SKIP ends it at any time, and HOW TO PLAY replays it. Enemies take it easy on you while the tutorial runs.

**TRAINING GROUND** is a solo sandbox for trying spells. Pick any hero; it arrives at level 12 with the first spell of every tier and its ultimate, facing a training dummy that never falls and never strikes back (it springs back to its post after knockbacks, tornadoes and freezes). **LOADOUT** (top right, or L) equips any basic attack, Low, Medium and High spell and switches each spell's three upgrades on and off. It also sets the level (1–20), switches between 1, 6 and 12 dummies, and turns the automatic basic attack off so spells can be measured alone. Cooldowns are real so the damage numbers mean something, but closing the loadout makes every spell ready again, the ultimate included; COOLDOWNS: OFF lets every spell fire nonstop. The bar at the top shows damage per second over the last five seconds, total damage and the biggest hit; tap it to reset. Nothing in the training ground counts toward a run.

## Spells and progression
Every hero holds **one basic attack, one Low, one Medium and one High spell, and one Ultimate**. Each is chosen once, from every spell of that tier, and kept for the whole run; nothing is ever replaced.

- **Before the first wave** you pick a hero, its basic attack (it fires by itself at the nearest foe) and its Low spell (button 1).
- **Levels 5, 9 and 12** open the Medium, High and Ultimate tiers. When that floor is cleared, the cards show every spell of the new tier: Medium goes on button 2, High on button 3, the Ultimate on its own button.
- **Every other cleared floor** (on boss floors: after the boss) offers three upgrades for spells you hold, basic attack included. Each spell has three upgrades; damage, size and duration upgrades multiply, extra projectiles or targets add up. Unpicked cards pick themselves after 18 seconds.
- **Levels** heal 35% and raise health and damage. Ignis, Kazimir, Seraphine, Nerida and Aldric take a new form at level 10, Voltaire at 12; Brandt and Mossa keep theirs.
- **Ultimates** have a 60-second cooldown and nothing else brings them back; a newly learned one is ready at once. Only one ultimate can run at a time: while a friend's is going, yours shows WAIT, so everyone gets to see each one.
- The SPELLBOOK (button, the hero frame, or B) shows all five tiers with every spell of the hero: yours lit with its upgrades, the rest dimmed, locked tiers with the level they open at. Tap a spell for details. The battle pauses while it is open.

The game is always landscape. On a phone held upright it is drawn sideways, so just turn the phone (this works with rotation lock on).

**AUTO MOVE** walks the hero for you; you still dodge, and dragging takes over for a moment. **AUTOPILOT** walks and dodges by itself: it steps out of telegraphed slams, sidesteps shots about to land and breaks out when surrounded and badly hurt. **AUTO SPELLS** casts your spells for you. The spell buttons always work: tapping one fires it at once, whatever MOVEMENT and SPELLS are set to. If you don't touch anything for 10 seconds during a fight, autopilot with auto spells takes over until you do. MENU pauses a solo game; in co-op the fight goes on.

Tap once for sound. On Android the game also goes fullscreen and big hits vibrate.

## Co-op (up to 4 heroes)
Choose **HOST CO-OP** on the menu to open **The Cellar Door**, the lobby: a 4-letter room code, **SEND INVITE LINK**, and four seats that fill as friends arrive, each showing the hero that player picked. Friends tap the invite link, or **JOIN CO-OP** and type the code on the letter pad. When everyone is ready the host taps **DESCEND**; each player then picks a basic attack and a first spell. Friends who arrive later drop straight into the fight. A room can also be opened mid-run from **MENU → PLAY TOGETHER**.

- XP and levels are shared; each player makes their own picks after every floor and boss, and picking never pauses the fight.
- A fallen hero comes back after 9 seconds while anyone is still standing.
- If a guest's connection drops, their hero fights on by itself and they get it back when the page reconnects. The host's phone runs the game, so it must stay open in the foreground.
- The connection indicator under the top bar shows round trip, packet loss and sync state; tap it for details.
- Matchmaking uses the free public PeerJS server; phones then talk directly, or through a relay when a network blocks direct connections. Co-op runs on the GitHub Pages / Home Screen version, not inside claude.ai.

## Cellar of Ruin II (the successor, in progress)
A dungeon crawl in `crawler/` (on GitHub Pages at `/crawler/`), its own game: deliberate real-time combat, a dark dungeon you light yourself, and roguelite runs. It shares only the engine with the first game (pixel renderer and lighting, the dungeon builder and its wall logic, sprites, effects, sound).

- **The heroes' weapons, cut by hand.** Each hero's weapon is cut out of his sprite and swung on its own. Where a frame has been cut by hand in the Hero Cut Painter (an artifact: the weapon's pixels marked, the hero redrawn where it was, its grip set), the game uses that (`node tools/herocuts.js <dir>` turns the painter's saved frames into `11_herocuts.js`); any other frame is cut by the weapon's colours as before. The Lancer and the Gladiator are cut by hand.
- **The walls as painted** (`31_walls`). Every wall piece of the cellar sheet is painted pixel by pixel in the Wall & Trim Painter (an artifact; its text goes into the game with `node tools/paintwalls.js painted.json`). Each pixel is **wall**, where no foot ever stands, or else **floor**, and it may also be **trim**: the ceiling, drawn over everything (units, blood, sparks, glows). The camera looks north and down, so the top of a wall is drawn north of where the wall stands. A south wall's whole row is floor behind it, under its trim: you walk in behind the wall and it hides you, all but your head. An east or west wall is wall under its trim, with a strip of floor four pixels wide beside the room: you walk right up to its top, which covers whatever of you reaches past it. A north wall's bricks are wall without trim: you stand in front of them, drawn over them, but your shadow, blood and anything else lying flat never are (its top trim only keeps the glows off it). A piece's floor is floor only where the floor reaches it, pixel by pixel from the floor tiles beside it; a foot there counts as standing on that floor tile, sight and shots pass over it, and coins never slide in under the ceiling. The floor behind a wall is whatever lies north of it: behind a pit or a pool the row is pit or water too, so nobody walks round a chasm or a flooded way behind the wall, and anything that blocks a corridor running east and west blocks that row as well. A pillar stands on a plinth: feet coming at it from the north stop where the plinth's top begins, never sinking in behind it, and the plinth is low, so whoever stands beside it, and his weapon, is drawn over it; the column above it is in front of or behind him by its middle.
- **The dungeon is dark.** Your lamp and the wall torches light it; what you have seen stays faintly visible, what you have not stays black, and foes only show where you can see them. A map in the corner fills in as you go. The way down is in the farthest room.
- **Combat is by hand.** Tap ATTACK to strike and keep tapping to chain the weapon's combo; hold it to charge a heavy blow. ROLL tumbles you out of the way, untouchable for most of it, and held on with a direction it becomes a sprint (half as fast again, costing 33 stamina a second, your footfalls pounding seven a second); BLOCK soaks blows for stamina, and raised just as a blow lands it parries, leaving the attacker staggered and open to a riposte. Every move costs stamina; spend the very last of it and you are winded: nothing comes back for 1.6 s, you can only trudge (40% speed, picking up as the stamina returns), you breathe hard through the nose, and sweat runs down your head and drips onto the stone. Holding block, the hero holds his weapon across his front just inside the guard's arc, between himself and it. Hitting a sleeping or unaware foe is a sneak attack; hitting one from behind is a backstab. Three flasks heal.
- **Kits:** every hero's weapon is cut out of his own sprite (found by its colours) and really swung: drawn back, swept through the arc, thrust, spun, slammed, raised back to charge, turned about the hand with RotSprite so the blade keeps clean pixels. Sellsword (sword and shield), Lancer (spear and shield), Gladiator (two-handed axe), Cutthroat (twin daggers, rolls far, deadly from behind). Each weapon has its own combo and heavy blow.
- **Foes** fight in four ways and together. Rushers (grunts, skitterers) run straight in and swarm. Tanks (brutes) throw you back with a wide shove, every third blow a slam. Squads: each tank shields up to three archers (shared out evenly); the tank stands between you and its archers and holds its ground (it steps across to stay in your way, never back, and shoves you off when you try to get past to them), the archers keep their distance in a loose line behind it and take turns to shoot (a steady stream, aimed a little ahead of you), and a second squad takes up a bearing a quarter turn round from the first, so the arrows come from two sides. Archers and casters with no tank work as a pair: they hold bearings a quarter turn apart round you (going round you to get there), so you cannot face both at once; a third or fourth takes the next quarter. Their arrowheads glint as they fly, a bright streak that twinkles a few times a second, even beyond the lamp's reach. Archers loose only along a clear line (pillars stop arrows exactly where their stone is drawn). Flankers circle wide to your back while you are busy with the others, stab and get out; turn to face them and they peel off. Bats fly: they hover over their shadows, rising and sinking gently and drifting in slow loops, turn in wide arcs, climb as they wind up and dive at you to strike; asleep they rest on the floor and take off when woken, and killed they fall out of the air. Every attack is marked on the floor in red before it lands. Awake foes see ahead of them and hear all round; sleepers lie on their backs and only hear, and spring up when startled; a foe that loses you searches where it last saw you and in the end goes home. Each floor is held by one army, named on the floor's card, and every foe on it comes from that army; an army that lacks a role fields more of its others. The cellars (floors 1–3): the goblin warband, the cellar vermin (no archers) or the ant colony (no flankers). The crypt (4–6): the bone legion (its brute a grappler mummy) or the rot, which often sends its mutilated stumblers as a horde of four to six that never sleeps. The deep (7 on): the lizardfolk tribe, the merfolk raiders, the elven host or the demon horde (its brute the blood drinker). The armies are laid out on an army board (a separate page with every creature of the Creature Compendium on it) and copied into the game from there. A floor is never held by the same army as the one above, where the depth has another. Every treasure room has a guardian standing by its chest: the scarecrow in the cellars, a stone or clay golem deeper down. Kept in reserve, on no floor yet: the king's soldiers, the wanderers (the minotaur and the toxic hound), the cackling pack (gnolls), the tinkers' guild (gnomes and a mithril construct), the plague nest (ratkin and their rats), the kobold warren and the skullsplitter clan (orcs).
- **The hero's panel** (top left): his portrait in a little lamp-lit frame (a close-up of his face, four times over, found from his sprite and breathing in its idle frames), his name in the display face, his health and stamina as bevelled bars with a notch every 25 health and the part just lost lingering pale before it drains, and his flasks as the flask from the item sheet, full or empty. Low on health the frame and the bar beat red with his heart; cursed, the stamina bar turns violet; out of breath, it flashes.
- **A new floor** starts with the hero walking down out of the stairway in the start room's north wall into the room. The way down has a dead torch on each side of it (the sheet's cold sconce); as a hero comes near the stairs they catch one after the other with a whoomp and a spray of sparks, flare and burn. The north walls carry the sheet's banners, the twelve tall ones and its eight small shields, between the torches, barred windows and vents.
- **Sound** wakes on any tap and whenever you come back to the game (an iPhone stops a page's sound when you switch apps and lets it start again only on a tap). If it stays silent, check the ring/silent switch: on silent an iPhone mutes web pages.
- **Sneaking.** Sleepers hear rather than see. Creep (push the invisible stick only a little, or hold C or Ctrl) and you can slip right past one: it wakes only if you bump into it. Walking wakes it from 34 px, rolling or fighting from 56. Creeping, the hero crouches (the upper body a pixel lower) and shades the lamp.
- **Stagger is not a chance.** Every foe has a hidden poise bar (grunt 55, archer 30, skitterer 12, brute 200) that each blow wears down (sword 10/10/24, heavy 60; spear 9/9/22, heavy 55; axe 28/36, heavy 95; daggers 4/4/4/10, heavy 24; sneak attacks, backstabs and ripostes ×1.5). It refills after 2 s without a hit; at zero the foe reels for 1.1 s and takes 30% more damage, and once back on its feet it cannot be worn down again for 2.5 s. Light blows rarely stagger on their own: finishers and heavies do. A parry always staggers, for 1.6 s. A brute cannot be worn down mid-slam. The hero has 30 poise, reels 0.45 s, then has 1.5 s of grace.
- **Blood.** It flies the way the blow went: a sword's sweep flings an arc of drops along the swing, a thrust (spear, a finisher, a dagger's stab) shoots a jet out of the far side, the axe throws heavy clots, daggers quick spurts, the overhead slam splashes all round; a foe's blow or an arrow bleeds the hero the same way. Half the drops fly as 2x2 clots (the axe throws more). Every drop lands as a soft blotch on the floor and stays. A drop that reaches a wall leaves a soft splash there (a north wall on its face, a side wall on its rim, a south wall against its edge); about half of them (nearly every clot) start a run that creeps down over 5 to 7 seconds, thinning, with a bead of blood at its front, and collects in a small blotch where it reaches the floor; a drop landing beside a run feeds it instead of starting another. Kills leave a pool. Each creature bleeds its own: red, green-yellow ichor (bugs), green slime (the maggot), black sludge (the crypt's dead flesh, the sand ghoul and the necromancer); skeletons, the scarecrow and the golems do not bleed (see below). Blood gathers on the hero's blade (patches from the tip down, in the victim's colour), drips from its tip and wears off over a minute; a roll wipes some away. Walk through a pool and you leave fading footprints. The badly wounded, hero or foe, leave a trail of drips. A wet squelch sits under every bleeding hit.
- **Overkill.** A killing blow that alone does more than half the foe's full health (whatever it had left) blows one of the bigger foes (archers, flankers, brutes; never the trash) apart: it bursts into chunks of its own sprite with raw red edges, which bleed as they fly and where they land, lie a few seconds and fade (no bodies stay). The moment is held: the hero freezes a little longer and the burst starts in slow motion before it flies.
- **Dying** (`48_deaths`). How he dies depends on what killed him, and each death plays out in silence with its own camera before the title comes up and the screen fades. **Bled out** (the plain one): after the killing blow he goes still on his feet, blood spurting from his chest with his heart; his legs go and he drops to his knees, letting his weapon fall; he reels there, swaying further each time; then he falls back, away from whoever killed him, and bleeds out, a spurt with every beat, each weaker, the beat slowing, the pool spreading; the camera pushes in on him slowly the whole while. **Blown apart**: killed by a heavy slam with gore at FULL. **Dissolved in acid**: killed by acid (a witch's flask, an ant's spit, a pool), he does not fall but sinks where he stands into the pool that took him, slowly then faster, shuddering now and then, the acid seething, bubbling and steaming round him with a sizzle; where he goes into it the acid eats him, the row of him at its surface fizzing yellow-green, every pixel flickering, the row above catching here and there, a froth seething on the surface round him; the pool stays till the screen fades; the camera pushes in. **Eaten by vermin**: dying with three or more vermin round him (rats, beetles, ticks, ants, cockroaches, maggots, the skitterers), enough of them between them to bury him (three rats will do, three ticks will not), he goes down and up to eight of them rush onto him and pile on, each where it hides most of what still shows of him, till not a pixel of him shows; they skitter in place but never so as to open a gap; they writhe there, chewing and crunching, blood squirting out of the heap; then they draw back, sated, and a skeleton lies in the pool (he turns to bones only in a frame where the heap hides every pixel of him and of the bones, tested as drawn, so never in view; his portrait keeps his own face). Foes near a dead hero stay in view. **The long fall**: going down a pit with nothing left to pay for it, he screams and the scream falls away with him; a long silence; then far below the impact and its echo back up the shaft, the camera held at the rim, leaning in over the dark. **Beheaded**: a heavy blow from a foe in melee (18 damage or more, or one time in four), with gore at FULL, takes his head off at the neck; it flies off, spinning and bleeding, and tumbles across the floor, the camera following it before it swings back to the body. The body stands headless and stumbles a few tiny steps while the neck jets a brutal amount of blood over the floor round it, a jet with every heartbeat, each weaker and further apart; then it drops to its knees, falls and bleeds out. The head is cut where the Head Cut Painter (an artifact: the head's pixels marked, the neck stump painted on the body) says for each hero's frame (`node tools/heads.js <dir>` turns its saved `heads` frames into `12_heads.js`); a frame not painted there is cut by the game (the top six rows over the face, a narrow raw stump under them). All four heroes' heads are cut by hand, every frame. (The older pieces of it: The killing blow throws a heavy spray; the hero reels, his weapon flies out of his hand and clatters across the floor (coming to rest lying flat, still bloodied if it was), he sinks to his knees and falls flat on his back, away from whoever killed him, on a long flat shadow of his own (blown apart, he casts none). Then he bleeds out over about six seconds: a jet of blood with each heartbeat, weaker and slower each time, the pool spreading out from under him while his lamp slowly dims (it still shows whoever is around him); the summary comes after about eight seconds. Killed by a heavy, long-telegraphed blow (a brute's slam; any attack that is a slam or winds up 0.8 s or more), with gore at FULL, he is blown apart instead.)
- **A brute's death throes.** Brought to 0 (and not blown apart), a brute does not drop at once: it stops, shaking and lurching, turning this way and that with a groan, blood spurting from its chest with every heartbeat while the pool under it spreads; it sinks to its knees and dies after about three seconds. Another blow finishes it at once. The scarecrow pours hay instead, a golem crumbles.
- **The bloodless.** Skeletons, the scarecrow and the golems shed bits instead of blood, flung the way the blow went just as blood is (the same arcs, jets and splashes for each weapon). Skeletons throw chips of bone; a blow that takes a quarter to a half of a skeleton's full health knocks a small bone loose half the time, a blow of half or more always does, a lighter one never, and it clatters across the floor; killed, a skeleton collapses into a heap of small bones and its skull rolls off; dry clacks and a rattle as it goes. A skeleton has four small bones and one skull: the bones knocked loose in the fight count towards them, and when it dies it spills all it has left, so it always leaves exactly five pieces (the skittering hand: no skull, two finger bones). The scarecrow throws out hay: loose stalks that flutter down slowly, swaying and turning over, and settle on the floor, with a soft whump and a rustle; a good blow knocks a tied bundle out, which tumbles and sheds stalks as it flies; in its death throes the hay pours out of it, and it dies in a cloud of hay and bundles. The golems spark and chip: pieces of their own stone break off and stay as rubble. The badly hurt leave a trail of stalks and chips. Loose stalks and chips stay on the floor like the blood; small bones, skulls and bundles lie six to nine seconds and fade. An overkill (gore at FULL) bursts the bigger ones into pieces of themselves as it does the bleeding: a skeleton into what it has left of its bones and its skull, flung hard, the scarecrow into tumbling chunks trailing hay, a golem into chunks of stone. None of the rest depends on the gore setting.
- **Water, wood and pixie dust.** The water elemental is a wet business: every blow throws a splash of water the way it went, each drop leaving a wet patch, every step it takes wets the floor, its blows soak the hero (he drips and leaves wet footprints for a while), its slam floods the floor around it, its death throes spill water and it dies in a burst that leaves a wide puddle; it all dries in time. The treant sheds splinters and leaves (they flutter down like the hay), most blows break off a twig (a hard one sometimes two), and it falls apart into logs. The pixie flies, trailing glowing pixie dust that drifts down twinkling; a blow knocks a puff of it out of her, with a chime, and she dies in a burst of it.
- **Spells.** The casters fight as the archers do (they keep their distance and line up a clear shot, marked by a red line as they aim), each with its own magic. The lizardfolk scout's rifle: a green glow gathers at the muzzle, then a fast, straight green laser bolt, one pixel thin. The acid ant spits a glob of green acid that drips as it flies and splashes where it lands. The goblin occultist's hex: a slow violet orb that wobbles and bends towards you and curses you: for four seconds your stamina comes back at a third of its pace and two of your standard blows in three go wide (a small violet MISS where one would have landed; a heavy blow still lands), the stamina bar turns violet, CURSED pops up over you in violet and violet motes rise off you. The vile witch lobs a poison flask in an arc at a spot marked on the floor (over the floor, never across a wall's corner: a flask that would catch a wall bursts against it); it shatters into a splash of bright green toxic sludge, painted the way blood pools are (wet blobs, deepest green under and brighter on top, so every spill has its own ragged edge; splashes thrown on the way the flask flew; bits of its glass lying in it), with bubbles swelling and popping, sheen drifting over it, fumes and a faint green light of its own; spent, it leaves a dark stain. It burns while you stand in it. You can tell it is the floor: each burn hisses and steams at your feet instead of drawing blood, its number is a sour yellow (healing is mint green, a blow red), the first burn from each pool pops ACID over you, the pool ripples out from your feet with every burn and seethes round them, and your health bar's edge burns green while you stand in it. The merfolk aquamancer sends a rolling wave of water that knocks you back and soaks you. The adept necromancer raises the dead: a green circle turns on the floor near you and a skeleton claws its way up out of it (four of his at most; come close to one with none and half the time he raises four round himself at once); with four up he throws bolts of green soul-fire that bend towards you. His raised dead give no gold and do not count as kills. The elf enchanter wards her own: a stream of green light puts a shimmer round one of them that takes three blows for it, fading as it wears, and breaks on the third (you see it go); her treant comes first: while it is near her without a ward, each time she can act it is even odds whether she wards it or throws a thorn, and she never throws more than two thorns before warding it again; anyone else she wards at most every six seconds, with a thorn after each ward (her healing is off for now). Bolts that fly straight (laser, hex, soul-fire, thorns) can be parried back; a wave or a flask cannot.
- **Set pieces made of the water** (level design, `37_levels`). A floor may hold any of three, chosen from the shape of its room graph. **The Flooded Way**: a room that the only way to the stairs runs through, flooded wall to wall but for a landing at each door. **The Gauntlet**: a room flooded but for a stone causeway carrying the corridors across it, with archers on small islands out of reach (two tiles of water away: not even a spear reaches) shooting at whoever crosses, and flooded pillars along the causeway to hide behind. **The Sentinel**: a five-tile pond in an ordinary room with a lone archer on an island in its middle, a turret. Every body of water that drains, the cisterns' too, has its **sluice in another room**, never beside it, and never found before its water: nothing is locked or switched off, the floor is laid out so that the room holding the sluice can only be reached through the water's room (a piece that can be crossed has rooms beyond it; the Flooded Way is put only in a room with a side branch, and a narrow stone ledge runs along its wall from the near door to that branch, so the sluice for the way on lies down the branch). The sluice is a pressure plate under an outlet grate in the north wall that trickles while its water is up. Stepping on it takes the view to that water as it drains (everything else holds still; attack or dodge skips it), then back. Once drained, the islands' archers can be reached and fight like any other. In the drained Flooded Way lie **the drowned**: undead half sunk in the mud, only their heads showing, who climb out when anyone comes near, the rest stirring after them. Some **corridors are barricaded** across with the sheet's wooden furniture (mine carts, crates, crate stacks, pots; all of it breaks, so a way can be smashed through; across a corridor running east and west the barricade fills the floor behind its south wall too), some are **cluttered** along both sides with the middle lane left clear, and smashed crates (the sheet's broken twins of its crates) lie about in them, still in the way. A tall crate (the sheet's crates two tiles high) is one thing and takes two blows: the first flashes it white and knocks a splinter or two off, the whole of it still standing; the second breaks all of it. A heap of crates two tiles wide and two high (in the storerooms) takes three. Pots burst into terracotta shards with a clay clink; crates and carts splinter with a wooden knock. A small chest (the sheet's two) opens at the first blow, its lid thrown up (the sheet's open twin beside it), and tosses out a little gold, now and then a flask; open and empty, the next blow splinters it. Every room has an **arrow in its floor** (the sheet's arrow slabs) pointing to the door that leads on toward the stairs. On the test rooms screen (TEST ROOMS, top right of the kit screen), **PUZZLE FLOOR** starts a run on a floor holding all three set pieces.
- **Puzzles that stand in the way.** From the second floor every floor has a puzzle between you and the stairs, and from the third two (one of each kind where they fit, the second's button sometimes only reachable once the first is solved); a floor that cannot take them is laid out again. Nothing is locked: the way itself is cut. **The Flooded Way** (above). **The drawbridge**: a chasm two or three tiles wide across a room the way down runs through (often the end room itself, between its doors and the stairs), wall to wall, with no way over it; its button (a raised plate like the sluices') is in a room of its own, down a side branch off the near side of the chasm when there is one, else in another room on this side. Stepping on it takes the view to the chasm while the sheet's wooden bridge is laid across it plank by plank from the near side, each with a knock and grit falling into the dark (attack or dodge skips ahead); a tile can be walked once both its planks are down. The test pilot steps on every button it has seen.
- **The shop** (`39_shop`, a prototype). From the second floor a dead end (one entered from the south, east or west) may be the shop: a small room laid out by hand as a scene. On the north wall two torches and a gold banner between them, his sign; against it his stock (crate stacks, pots, a small chest, a heap of crates); the shopkeeper (the overworked old villager, his idle slowed to a third) behind a long wooden counter of the sheet's crates, a brazier burning either side of him; on the counter his wares (a flask, a fish, the floor's map, a flask belt with room for one more flask, a gambeson), each with its price on a tag on the counter's front, and drawn over whoever stands at the counter (the counter stops a customer a few pixels short of it). Nobody fights in here: stepping in, the hero slings his weapon on his back (a sword blade down behind him, a spear or an axe head up over his shoulder), walks slowly (a small room full of things), and the view comes in a little closer; the dungeon's drone fades out altogether and he hears the torches and braziers crackle and his own slow steps on the stone. Standing before a ware shows its name and price; attack buys it: the coins go over the counter to him, the ware comes over to the hero, and the keeper has a word to say (he says so too when you cannot pay, your flasks are full, or you are hale already). What he says is typed out a letter at a time in pale blue, steady over his head and above the ware's name, so it reads as speech and not as a floating number. Foes stop at the door and their arrows die in it. The kit screen's test rooms have a SHOP with 150 gold to spend.
- **Bottomless pits** (`38_pits`). Holes in the floor going down into the dark, from the sheet's own pit pieces: the earth under a floor's edge with roots hanging from it, fading to black, lies in the pit right under a floor (we look down past the floor's edge at its thickness), or under the foot of a north wall the pit runs up to; the rest of a pit is black, the floor beside it is seen edge on and the floor in front of it hides its far side (a side wall's strip of floor beside a pit, and the top of a south wall in front of one, go down into it too). Feet stop at the edge (it blocks like water, and sight, arrows and spells pass over it), and so do rolls and lunges; but a hard enough blow carries whoever takes it over the edge: a brute's shove or slam (straight away from the brute, so you go over only with the pit behind you), a heavy swing, a wave, a spike trap's throw. Who goes over falls: smaller and darker into the black, turning a little, sinking behind the floor in front of the hole, with a rush of air and, far below, a few stones still falling. A hero climbs back out where he last stood clear of every pit, a fifth of his health the poorer and blinking a moment; a fall he cannot pay for is his last (he is simply gone). A foe that falls is gone, its loot with it (it counts as a kill). Blood, gibs, sparks and stones that come down over a pit drop away into it and leave no mark; nothing painted on the floor ever shows on the void. A shadow lies only on the floor: it stops at a pit's edge, never on the earth face or the black, and never on a wall either (a north wall's bricks stand up from the floor's edge, so whoever stands at their foot is drawn over them but his shadow is not). Where a wall beside a pit leaves pixels open (a side wall's strip, the open corner under a cap), they are pit too, the earth face carried on into them. Pressing at an edge knocks crumbs off it. A room can have them seven ways (never the first room, the last, the treasure room or a set piece's, and every door stays joined to every other): **the chasm**, a band of pit two or three tiles wide from wall to wall, crossed by a bridge two tiles wide (the floor left standing, or half the time the sheet's wooden bridge, its planks fresh or green with age) with a brute standing guard on it; **broken floor**, holes here and there with cracked slabs round some of them (a crumb drops off one now and then: the tell) that shake and grind under a foot and drop away a moment later, taking whoever stands on them; **the ledges**, a room all pit but a platform in its middle with a brute on it and a way two tiles wide out to it from every door; **the crumbling floor**, a room with no hole in it as you come in, nearly every slab of it cracked: each gives way a moment after a foot comes down on it, so whoever keeps moving gets across with the floor falling in behind him, and whoever stops goes down; a winding way of sound slabs joins every door, looking like the rest (only the crumbs that drop now and then off the cracked ones tell them apart). A treasure room may be laid the same way, looking like any other, its sound ways leading to the chest and a little sound floor in front of it for the guardian (until it comes after you). Two more only give a fight an edge: **pit corners**, a hole two or three tiles square in two to four of a room's corners, and **the pit rim**, a pit a tile wide along every wall but where the doors come in; a shove, a slam or a heavy swing near the walls is a fall. A treasure room may have either (its chest and its guardian's floor stay). **The spiked walkway** (from the second floor): all pit but a walkway two tiles wide from every door to a junction in the middle (an L where a door does not line up with it), with a field of spike traps two or three deep across one of the ways; a spike throws whoever it stabs hard out of the nearer end of its field, along the way, and from the third floor down the field lies right before a turn, so the throw out of that end carries on over the corner into the pit, and a brute holds the junction. Cracked slabs lie still until a hero first comes into their room, and a heavy blow's cracks (a brute's slam's too) running over one set it crumbling. The first floor may have broken floor, a crumbling floor or pit corners; deeper floors a chasm or the ledges, and broken or crumbling floor, pit corners or the rim besides. The minimap shows pits dark. On the test rooms screen, **PIT FLOOR** starts a run on a floor with all of them (and a crumbling treasure room).
- **The cellar's works.** Built from the cellar sheet's own pieces. Water takes any shape: its pieces are overlays, put together quarter by quarter (square-cut stone) or on the corners between tiles (the rounded and the deep sets), so a body of water can have bends, islands and channels. Feet cannot go in; sight, arrows and spells pass over it, and a drop of blood falling in leaves a ring. Some rooms have a pond or two in their corners (rounded or deep water, clear of the cross the corridors make through the room). One room on a floor may be a **cistern** (from the third floor a big one may be **a flooded store**, the water crowded with eight to twelve sunken things): a square-cut stone pool filling the room with a walk round it, a bridge across (two wide, the way its doors face: the sheet's stone slats, or half the time its wooden planks), a flooded pillar or two standing in it, crates, pots, carts, heaps, small chests and stone (columns, a statue on its pedestal, low blocks, slabs, cold braziers) standing in the water too (the sheet has every one of them a second time standing in water, its foot under the waterline; all of them are used but the carved rune slabs, which belong to the shrines), and its pressure plate in another room (its socket is a slab standing proud of the floor, three pixels, and the button stands two more in it until a foot drives it flush: a hero coming at it, pressed or not, checks for a moment and hops up onto it, drawn a little toward its middle on the way up; it sinks only once he has landed; whoever stands on it stands that much higher, his shadow on the slab with him, and stepping off he drops down with a little hop). Step on the plate and it sinks with a clunk, the water gurgles away down a small grate in a whirl over a few seconds, and the wet basin can be walked: as the water falls below them, the things standing in it (and the pillars) turn into their dry selves and can be broken (the stone stays: it never breaks; a small chest opens like any other), and some coins lie at the bottom, with now and then a fish or two flopping on the mud of a big one. The basin is a step down: anyone walking into it or out of it checks for a moment at the rim and gives a little hop over it. Where square-cut water runs right up to a south wall it runs on under the wall's top edge (from the north we look over that wall at the water, so neither the pool's south rim nor floor in front of it shows). The bridge's shadow lies on the water under it, dark between its slats and between its two rows. Blood falling in the water leaves a ring and a dark red haze that spreads and thins away; gibs, bones and stones thrown in splash and sink (flesh leaving a bigger haze). Blood (and anything else that marks the floor) stays on the bridge's slats where it lands on them; a drop that falls between them goes down through the gap, into the water or, once it has drained, onto the basin floor below, where it shows between the slats. One room may be a **rune shrine**: three dark rune plates in the floor and a chest in the middle walled in by eight stone blocks (it cannot be opened while they stand). From the second floor half the shrines are **dark storerooms**: their torches and braziers dead (the sheet's cold sconces and braziers, now and then with embers), the room crowded with crates, pots and stacks, and the rune plates under some of them; smash your way to them (a rune under its crate cannot be woken until the crate is broken). The last rune woken, the dead lights catch one after another round the room. A drawbridge's bridge may be laid by such a room's runes instead of a button: the view goes to the chasm a moment after the last one wakes. Each plate wakes as you step on it, a column of gold light shooting up, sparks and a ring, its own note (the three climb to a chord), and keeps glowing gold; when the third wakes, beams of light run from all three runes to the vault and the blocks sink into the floor one after another. From the second floor down, some corridors have a field of **spike traps** across them (now and then a trap is drawn as a cluster of three spikes, the sheet's own, working just the same), the corridor's width and four to six tiles deep, that shoot up together on a beat with a hiss of steel and a hard stop (SHHHUK) and stab anyone over them or stepping onto them while they are up, foe or hero (14 or so a stab, once a beat), throwing them hard off the field (harder than a brute's shove) out of its nearer end. Crossing one between two beats takes a quick walk at the right moment, or a sprint. The rooms are dressed from the sheet too: barred windows and vents in the brick between the banners, crates, crate stacks and heaps of sacks among the pots (they all break), and the brown floors have their own torches and braziers.
- **How some move.** The mutilated stumbler shuffles: slow lurching steps with a stop between them, swaying, dripping rot, groaning now and then as it goes (more often once it has seen you); it never sleeps, and idle it mills about aimlessly. The dismembered crawler moves at a flanker's pace, but its limbs scrabble at a quicker cadence than anyone's. The tunneling mole travels under the floor as a moving mound of earth that throws up dirt, leaves a furrow and drags a wake of heaped earth behind it, one unbroken ridge whichever way and however fast it goes, that sinks back from the tail; under the floor it cannot be hit. It bursts up to strike and stays up a moment after, open to a blow, then dives again.
- **When the hero falls** the foes near him lower their weapons, back away a few steps still facing him, and stand watching him die.
- **Armour & death room** (test rooms screen, first): the blood test room's room, its doors shut, the hero in a gambeson and a spare one lying by the west wall (back two seconds after it is taken). The foes fight for real, one lot at a time, coming on once the card has gone: grunts, a brute (its slam blows you apart), archers, a swarm of skitterers, flankers, acid ants, then all of them at once; NEXT at the top brings on the next lot, CLEAN wipes the blood (the pit stays), FOES sends them away and keeps them away till it is pressed again (or NEXT). Nothing heals him and he can die: his death plays out in full, then he gets up again where he began, whole, in a new gambeson, and the foes come back fresh a moment later. DIE at the top dies the next death on the list at once (bled out, the long fall into a pit dug by the east wall, blown apart, dissolved in acid, eaten by a swarm of vermin brought on for it, eaten by just three rats, the fewest that can bury him, beheaded), its name under the buttons.
- **Water test room** (test rooms screen): a floor with a cistern, its doors shut and nobody else on it, the hero by the drain plate, and the water holding as many of the things that stand in water as fit. Step on the plate to drain it; REFILL at the top brings the water back in from the grate, with everything that stood in it, and lifts the plate for another go (CLEAN wipes the blood, as in the other test rooms).
- **Creature test room** (test rooms screen): the same shut room with one creature or group at a time in it, awake and fighting for real: the water elemental, the treant, the pixies, the scouts' rifles, the stumblers, the crawlers, the moles, each caster's spell, the chest guards, then each army (one of each of its troops), then each army in reserve. NEXT at the top brings on the next (its name shows under it); a group that falls comes back after two seconds. The hero cannot die there and his health comes back on its own.
- **Blood test room** (test rooms screen: TEST ROOMS at the top right of the kit screen lists the puzzle floor, the pit floor and the three test rooms, played as the kit picked there): one room with its doors shut (blood thrown at a doorway splashes on the door and runs down it), eight foes in its corners and at the middle of each wall that neither move nor fight back, each back 1.5 s after it dies as a creature of the same kind from any army or a chest guard (so every kind of blood turns up); the CLEAN button at the top wipes all the blood away.
- **Gore setting** (pause menu): FULL, BLOOD (no bursts) or OFF; remembered on the device.
- **Lettering** (title screen or pause menu): CLASSIC, or NEW (TRIAL), a two-face set on trial: Grenze Gotisch keeps the headings, and every other word (buttons, HUD labels, hints, kit cards) is set in Cinzel; the numbers (floating numbers, health, gold, prices) in Cellar Numerals, a copy of Grenze Black with its level digits made the default (`crawler/assets/fonts`, SIL Open Font License); and the game's name in Pirata One on the title screen. Off by default; remembered on the device.
- **Things to smash.** Pots, barrels, crates and chests break with one blow and sometimes hold gold, a fish (heals 15% at once) or a flask (gives back a flask charge); both are rare finds: a pot, barrel or crate about one in seventy times for a fish and one in a hundred and twenty for a flask, a small wooden chest a flask one in thirty (never a fish); the treasure chests are where they are found. **The gambeson** (quilted cloth armour; its icon is armours [0, 5] of the 16x16 Assorted RPG Icons, its stark black outline recoloured to the item sheet's soft charcoal: new items never bring pure black in, see below): forty points of armour that take each blow before his health does, and are hacked away doing it. A blow on it spills no blood: its padding and scraps of its linen fly out the way the blow went, in about the scarecrow's measure of hay, and the number struck comes up in the cloth's colour. On the HUD, in pixel art in the gambeson sprite's own colours (padded channels stitched every four pixels), a thick band of quilting wraps his health bar over its edges and pads his portrait's frame while he wears it; it is cut back from the right to a ragged edge as the armour goes, each piece hacked off falling away. What a blow has left once the cloth is gone goes on to his health; the last of it gone, the gambeson is torn off. A quarter of the treasure chests hold one while his is not whole, and the shop sells one (+40 armour, 40 gold and 8 more a floor); either puts a whole one on him. One small wooden chest on each floor (not in the room you come down into, if it can be helped; a floor with none gets one) holds **the floor's map** (a parchment with the floor plan on it, its maze repainted by the player in the Item Painter): picked up, the whole floor is remembered at once, the minimap with it, and only then does the minimap mark the way down (in yellow; a dark violet dot always marks where you came down), and the hero holds it up over his head a moment with a small gold AHA!. Each floor has one treasure chest (in the treasure room, or else in a guarded room; coming at it from the north your feet go a pixel or two in behind its lid): one blow throws it open and it stays open, tossing its gold and goods out of its front one after another so they lie spread on the floor; the coins wait a moment before they fly to you. Black, blue, red and gold chests hold more the deeper you go (red and gold give both a fish and a flask). A fish or flask you do not need lies on the floor for later. Ore rocks (the crystal and gold geodes) are no longer placed in the cellar; the code that breaks them (three blows, a white flash, chips and a glint, then a burst into chunks of their own pixels) stays for a later theme such as the mines. For now they are only for show.
- **Hit-stop is local.** A blow that lands holds the one struck (shivering) and the one striking for a moment; the rest of the fight never pauses, except briefly on a parry.
- Coming next: loot (weapons, armour, trinkets), tomes and modifiers for magic, bosses, unlocks between runs, co-op. Keys: WASD, C or Ctrl creep, J attack, Space roll, Shift block, F flask, Esc menu; the mouse aims.

A running game checks the site for a newer build every minute: the menus reload into it by themselves; during a run the HUD says UPDATE READY and the pause menu offers the update.

## Art rules (Cellar of Ruin II)

- **No pure black.** The game's art has no stark black: outlines are soft and tinted (the item sheet's `#1d1d1c` charcoal on the fish and flask, browns on the chests, a navy on the map). An item or icon brought in from another pack has its black outline (and any other near-pure black) recoloured to the item sheet's charcoal, or to the tinted dark that suits it, before it goes on a sheet; procedural art (the HUD's quilting) uses the same charcoal.
- **Pixel art at whole pixels.** Anything drawn for the game, HUD included, is drawn in art pixels on a grid and scaled up without smoothing, in colours taken from the sprites it belongs with, never as smooth gradients or hairlines.

## Run it
Open `index.html` (it is self-contained), or add the GitHub Pages site to your Home Screen. The sequel is `crawler/index.html`.

## Build
```
python3 -I tools/pack.py <extracted cellar tileset zip> <folder with every extracted creature pack> .
python3 -I tools/add_mobs.py <extracted Compendium/Animations folder> . Name [Name ...]   # add Compendium creatures to the sheet
python3 -I tools/recolor.py   # vampire, priest, tidecaller, stormcaller, general and their evolved forms, the general's archers and riders, angels (palette swaps)
node tools/build.js
node tools/build.js crawler   # the sequel
```
`src/*.js` is concatenated into `index.html` (Pages / Home Screen) and `dist/artifact.html`; `crawler/src/*.js` into `crawler/index.html` and `crawler/dist/artifact.html` (it also inlines `crawler/assets/cellar_doors.png`).
Checks: `node tools/shots.js`, `node tools/scenes.js`, `node tools/soak.js` against a local server on port 8787; `node tools/soak2.js <seconds> <turbo> <kit>` plays the successor with its test pilot.

Art: the Dungeon Cellar tileset and its doors by ELV Games (rooms and props), and the 16x16 creature packs by Deep Dive Game Studio: Magical, Humanoid, Humanoid II, Vermin, Undead, Demon, Dragon and Holy, and more creatures from their Creature Compendium (heroes, monsters and angels; some heroes are palette swaps of their sprites).
